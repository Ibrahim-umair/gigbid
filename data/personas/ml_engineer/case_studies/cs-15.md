The founder of a small trading research community followed four busy Telegram channels and missed the few messages that mattered among roughly 600 a day. I built a triage service that reads those channels through a Telethon user session, classifies each message with an LLM (actionable idea, news, chatter, promotion) and forwards only the actionable ones, with a two-line summary and the original link, to a private channel.

The founder supplied 400 messages she had labelled herself. I used 150 as few-shot examples in the prompt and kept 250 for evaluation: precision on the actionable class reached 0.89 and recall 0.94. Duplicates and cross-posts are collapsed with an embedding similarity check, which removed about 22% of forwards. Spam and promotional links are dropped before they reach the model, which also reduces cost.

Monthly spend on the language model is about $9. A weekly digest message lists what was forwarded and what was suppressed, so the founder can correct the labels and retrain the prompt examples. The service runs in a small Docker container on a $6 virtual server and restarts itself after Telegram session drops.
