A small health insurer kept claims data in three PostgreSQL databases that disagreed on member identifiers and date formats. The analytics lead wanted a single reporting layer. I wrote ingestion scripts in Python that pulled 2.3 million claim lines nightly, standardised identifiers and dates with pandas, deduplicated by a documented set of rules, and loaded the result into BigQuery with partitioned tables.

Data quality came first. I added 38 automated checks (null rates, impossible dates, negative amounts, member counts per plan) and a daily summary email. These checks exposed 1.7% duplicate claim lines that had been inflating a monthly cost figure by about $140,000 in one report.

On top of the clean tables, I built a dashboard with claims cost per member per month, top diagnosis groups, provider outlier detection and lag between service date and payment. Reporting that previously needed a two-day manual extraction was available by 7 am each day. Access was restricted at the view level, and personal identifiers were hashed in the analytics dataset. The engagement lasted six weeks and finished with a handover session and a written runbook.
