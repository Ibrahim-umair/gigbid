A 60-lawyer firm kept precedent notes, know-how memos and matter checklists scattered across a wiki, shared drives and exported chat threads. Associates asked partners the same questions repeatedly. I built an internal assistant that searches these sources and answers with links to the underlying document.

The hard part was permissions. A note from a confidential matter must never surface for someone outside that matter team. Each chunk carries an access-control list copied from its source, and retrieval filters on the asking user's groups before anything reaches the model. I tested this with a red-team set of 80 adversarial questions designed to leak restricted content; none leaked. LangChain was used for the retrieval and routing logic, and answers are refused when the best passage scores below a calibrated threshold.

On a 150-question evaluation written by senior associates, 88% of answers were judged correct and well-sourced, 9% were partial, and 3% were wrong or unsupported. Median response time was 3.4 seconds. In the pilot with 14 associates, partner interruptions for know-how questions fell by about half, and the firm extended the pilot to the whole litigation group.
