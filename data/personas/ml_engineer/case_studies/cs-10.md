A freight-forwarding software company ran five document models (a classifier for document type, two token-classification extractors, a language detector and a summariser) and had given each its own GPU box, most of them idle overnight. They asked for one service that could host all of them on a single 24 GB GPU.

I wrapped the models, all pulled from the Hugging Face hub and fine-tuned for their documents, in an asynchronous service. Requests are accepted immediately, placed on a Celery queue backed by Redis, and results are fetched by job id or pushed by webhook. A small registry loads and unloads models on demand, with a configurable schedule (for example, the summariser only stays resident from 08:00 to 18:00), an LRU eviction rule and a memory budget that refuses to load a model if it would exceed 21 GB.

Median latency was 120 ms for classification and p95 was 410 ms for extraction under 40 concurrent requests. Four GPU instances were retired, cutting infrastructure cost by 41%, roughly $2,300 a month. Prometheus counters expose queue depth and load/unload events, and a simple admin page lists which models are resident.
