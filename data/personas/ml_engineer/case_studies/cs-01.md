A network of eleven outpatient clinics kept its compliance and HR rules in roughly 1,400 PDFs, and front-desk staff phoned the compliance office about 90 times a week to find answers. I built a retrieval-augmented assistant over those documents. Files were split by heading structure rather than fixed token windows, embedded, and stored in PostgreSQL with pgvector. LangChain handled the retrieval chain, with a reranking step and a strict "answer only from the passages, otherwise say you don't know" prompt. Every answer carries the document title, page and effective date.

To judge quality I wrote 200 real staff questions with reference answers and scored groundedness and citation correctness by hand on a first pass, then with an LLM grader calibrated against that sample. Groundedness reached 91% and citation accuracy 94%. The p95 response time was 2.8 seconds and the average cost was about $0.012 per question.

Within eight weeks of launch, calls to the compliance office fell by 62%. The pilot clinic reported that new hires found answers without asking a colleague in 7 out of 10 cases. The documents are re-ingested nightly, so superseded policies drop out of search automatically.
