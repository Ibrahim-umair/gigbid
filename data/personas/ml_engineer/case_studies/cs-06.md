A digital lending start-up needed the selfie step of its onboarding flow to reject printed photos and replayed videos. I built a service in two stages. First a face detector returns the bounding box and landmarks and checks visibility (eyes and mouth unobstructed, face size, brightness, blur). Second, a liveness stage combines a challenge (blink or turn head, chosen at random per session) with a texture classifier trained to separate live skin from screen and paper.

The training set combined 12,000 public liveness frames with 1,800 clips the client's testers recorded by deliberately attacking the system with phone replays and glossy prints. On the hold-out set the system rejected 96% of replay attacks and 98% of print attacks, with a false rejection rate for genuine users of 1.8%. Processing took about 180 ms per frame on CPU, so no GPU was needed in production.

Both stages are exposed as REST endpoints that the client's mobile team calls from their app. Every decision stores a reason code, which cut manual KYC reviews by about 40% in the first month. We did not store raw frames beyond 24 hours.
