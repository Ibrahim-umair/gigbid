An online home-goods store with 9,000 SKUs had a support inbox full of questions that the product pages already answered: dimensions, materials, compatibility, delivery windows. I added a chat widget to the storefront that streams answers token by token over WebSockets. Product records, size charts and the returns policy are embedded and indexed in Pinecone, with metadata filters for category and stock status so the assistant never recommends something sold out.

The widget's backend is a small Python service that rewrites the shopper's message using recent turns, retrieves the top passages, and calls the model with a short system prompt tuned for the brand's tone. Order-status questions are routed to a fixed lookup against the order API, never to the model, which removed a class of hallucinated tracking answers we saw in testing.

After a four-week A/B test on 30% of traffic, 54% of chats ended without a human ticket, average satisfaction rose from 4.4 to 4.6 out of 5, and the support team's backlog shrank by about 35%. Monthly inference cost stayed under $210 at roughly 14,000 conversations.
