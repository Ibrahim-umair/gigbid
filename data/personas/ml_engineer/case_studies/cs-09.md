A small online retailer ran its Monday trading review from six spreadsheets exported by hand, which took the operations lead about five hours and was error-prone. I replaced this with a set of 14 SQL queries against the shop's PostgreSQL replica, a pandas layer for cohort and margin calculations, and an interactive Plotly dashboard refreshed every night.

The dashboard covers revenue by channel, margin by SKU after returns and shipping, repeat-purchase cohorts, and stock cover. While validating the numbers, I noticed that the return rate for one supplier's range sat at 11% against 3% for the rest of the catalogue; it traced back to a sizing chart error on 23 product pages. Fixing it brought that supplier's returns back to 4%, worth roughly $1,900 a month.

The weekly report now takes about ten minutes of reading rather than five hours of assembly. I documented every metric definition in a short data dictionary, so the team knows, for instance, that "net revenue" excludes VAT and gift-card redemptions. The project was done as a fixed-price engagement over three weeks.
