A fintech helpdesk offered customers an assistant by phone: speech goes to a transcription model, then a language model, then a voice synthesiser. Median time to first audio was 2.9 seconds, and callers kept talking over the bot. I profiled each stage with timestamps on 500 recorded calls and found the time split roughly 0.9 s for end-of-speech detection and transcription, 1.4 s waiting for the full LLM reply, and 0.6 s for synthesis.

Three changes made the difference. First, a tuned voice activity detector with a 350 ms trailing silence cut the transcription wait. Second, I streamed LLM tokens and started speech synthesis on the first complete sentence rather than the whole reply. Third, I cached short, frequent phrases such as acknowledgements and the "let me check that" filler, and ran transcription with a smaller faster-whisper model for calls with clear audio.

The median time to first audio fell from 2.9 s to 1.4 s, and p90 from 4.7 s to 2.6 s. Caller interruptions ("barge-in") dropped by 44%, and the word error rate rose by only 0.6 points. The work took nine days, and the client's own telephony code was left untouched.
