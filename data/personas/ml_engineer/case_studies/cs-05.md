A distribution centre wanted automatic counts of pallets leaving six loading docks, to reconcile against dispatch notes. I trained a YOLOv8 model on 2,300 hand-labelled frames from the existing IP cameras (day, night, partial occlusion by forklifts) and added a tracker with a virtual counting line.

The first deployment dropped frames badly: the detector was reading the RTSP stream in the same loop as inference, so the buffer grew and the video skipped several seconds at a time. I split capture into its own thread with a one-slot queue that always keeps the newest frame, reconnect logic for stalled streams, and half-precision inference. Throughput per stream went from 14 to 25 frames per second on a single RTX 3060 handling two docks, with no visible skipping.

Accuracy on a week of manually counted footage was 97.5% (mAP50 of 0.93 on the validation set). Discrepancies between dispatch notes and actual pallets were flagged automatically, and the client reported catching 31 mis-shipments in the first two months. Counts are written to PostgreSQL and shown on a simple dashboard.
